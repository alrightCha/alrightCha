Satoshi once said that Bitcoin will either go to zero or go to millions. He was wrong. It will go to millions and to zero. Here is the game theory and economics behind it.

The halving trap

Bitcoin is capped at 21 million coins, going through a halving of rewards to miners every four years. Compute solving mathematical puzzles to get rewarded is the reason your transactions go through without being compromised. And the appealing reward is what keeps it decentralized, as a certain equilibrium is formed among the miners: the higher the reward, the more room there is for everyone to eat, the bigger the network.

The reverse is equally true. The smaller the reward, the less room there is for everyone to eat, the smaller the network gets.

We are already seeing the room getting smaller.

The GPU exodus

AI and blockchain both run on GPUs. One is more profitable than the other. For this reason, we are seeing many OG Bitcoin miners go the other route. For them to come back, Bitcoin needs to reach higher prices.

We can already assume that Bitcoin relies on reaching higher prices to maintain its decentralization, which is the sole reason why Bitcoin is Bitcoin. This is inherently a flaw in and of itself, but let us assume that Bitcoin's price will keep growing. Which it will, because it makes sense mathematically: the more fiat there is circulating, while maintaining relevance, the bigger Bitcoin will in fact grow.

This is the first step of it going to millions.

The road to zero

But Bitcoin will eventually go to zero. That, or it will totally replace the denominator.

Somewhere around 2104, there will be zero Bitcoin issuance. So what does this mean for the miners? They will rely 100% on transaction fees to get rewarded.

We have already established that Bitcoin needs to grow exponentially in price to remain decentralized. This also means that Bitcoin's transaction fees will grow exponentially when valued by the dollar.

What is also interesting is that the economics of Bitcoin make it a store of value from the get-go. A store of value is stored. It is rarely transacted. This is why we see many wallets dormant for years. The usual "buy Bitcoin, place it in cold storage and forget about it for years".

So what does this mean? It means that transaction fees are not a rational solution to rewarding the miners. They will never be enough to keep the system decentralized. This is yet another fundamental flaw.

ETFs: fuel on the fire

The fuel that turns this from a tree burning into the whole Amazon forest are the derivatives. Precisely, Bitcoin ETFs.

Centralized exchanges need to hold some Bitcoin, to say the least, for them to work. Any user can withdraw their Bitcoin at any time off-chain, meaning exchanges need to provide real Bitcoin on-chain. This is why centralized exchanges hold enormous balances on-chain and are most of the time top holders. They keep transactions flowing. They contribute to the sanity of Bitcoin. While using BTC purely off-chain and through self-custody is the way it was intended to be used, centralized exchanges still remain in touch with Bitcoin's economy. They are just a layer of abstraction.

ETFs, however, are another order of magnitude of abstraction.

Unless imposed legally and through audits, ETFs do not even need to buy Bitcoin at all. They only represent themselves as a layer on top of it, but the full economy they bring is 100% off-chain. Even worse, they profit from the volume instead of Bitcoin. They take away those amassed fees that were supposed to keep Bitcoin decentralized by rewarding the miners.

Where does this leave us?

I think the image is clear.

If Bitcoin will remain relevant, it will need massive cooperation among humanity to choose Bitcoin and use it as it was intended.

If it keeps being pushed by the government, the government will decide its outcome, which will most likely be a few mining centers maintained by the state, operating at a loss: a lot of power needed to keep it secure, and tiny profits as there are no block rewards and negligible fees. Bringing Bitcoin back to centralization and rendering the whole vision of a decentralized currency to ashes.

The only way forward

The only way forward is to break the flaws of Bitcoin. The computers need to do something beneficial and become an asset in and of themselves. Solving random mathematical puzzles that do not benefit anyone is a waste. It is what makes Bitcoin rely on its price to grow for it to work as intended.

The protocol and consensus mechanism need to revolve around something that turns a liability (solving puzzles) into an asset: training AI models, distributing AI, distributing compute, whatever pays more, whatever the market demands.

Therefore, there needs to be a market for what these computers are needed for, and their computation needs to be turned into a proof of work, for an idea like Bitcoin to truly work.
