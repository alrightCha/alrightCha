Determining the legitimacy of an event or a product is subjective.

We as humans rely mostly on how an event is viewed, rather than what it actually does, to determine its worthiness.

This could be seen as the science of the human mind, but in a technocratic environment which we are leaning more towards, it involves much more.

Here are a couple examples that come to mind:

  • A new crypto project is out. You don't know whether or not you want to invest in it. The first thing you do is check who follows it instead of what it does.
  • A new app is out. You don't know the developer. You don't know if you can truly rely on the platform being secure or not. What you rely on is the rating of the app itself. Made by users.
  • An event is around the corner. You don't know the artists. The first thing you check is who is participating in the event.

What is wrong with the current setup ?

As we move forward in the age of AI, we won't be able to tell the difference between a human or a bot interaction.

This poses major risks of proving what is worthy. What is even real ?

Am I talking to a human ? Are you reading words written by a person ?

How can the internet remain relevant to our daily lives if we continue moving in this direction ?

The internet is all about its users. It is a zero-sum game. There are no winners in it. Having the internet just to yourself renders it worthless other than being a file-storage system you can access from anywhere.

The internet is all about interaction. You read other's posts. You share your feedback to others. You visit websites made by others. Finding a way to differentiate a bot from a human is more than necessary on the brink of this AI revolution.

I imagine a social network that is decentralized where people have an influence score based on prior interactions in the space.

With a consensus, one can determine how valuable a human's input is. Or how "seriously" a person should be taken. This is not about placing a human more than another, but how impactful one is among a space of interest, making one's impact proportional to their input to a space. This can of course be optional, depending on how founders choose to define the voting or impact a contributor to be. Of course this would resolve the bot problem, and would bring more rational voting to the table.
